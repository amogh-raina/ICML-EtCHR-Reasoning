**Case Summary:**
The applicant is a journalist who was covering a political demonstration. During the event, police officers tried to take her camera, used force against her (grabbing/pulling her hands), arrested her, and took her to the police station where her camera and memory cards were seized. She claims this obstructed her journalistic work.

**Assessment Steps:**

1. **Existence of an interference:**
   - The applicant was a journalist covering an event (para 5-6)
   - Police officers tried to pull her camera from her hands (para 8)
   - She was arrested and taken to police station (para 8)
   - Her camera and memory cards were seized (para 9)
   - This clearly interfered with her ability to gather and disseminate information as a journalist
   - Therefore: **Yes, there was interference with her Article 10 rights**

2. **Lawfulness of the interference:**
   - Need to check if interference had basis in domestic law
   - She was arrested on suspicion of hooliganism (para 9)
   - Criminal proceedings were opened for hooliganism (para 11)
   - Police Act (section 30) allows use of force in certain circumstances (para 41)
   - So the interference appears to have a legal basis in Armenian law
   - Therefore: **The interference was lawful** (proceed to step 3)

3. **Legitimate aims:**
   - The interference was justified as:
     - Maintaining public order (she was in an altercation, para 7-8)
     - Preventing crime (hooliganism charges, para 11)
     - These are listed legitimate aims under Article 10(2): "prevention of disorder or crime", "public safety"
   - Therefore: **The interference pursued legitimate aims**

4. **Necessary in a democratic society:**
   - Need to assess if interference was:
     a) Pressing social need
     b) Least restrictive measure
     c) Convincing and sufficient reasons

   Key considerations:
   - She was a journalist, identifiable as such (she claimed to show press card, para 15)
   - The video shows police pulling at her camera while she shouted "I'm a journalist" (para 8)
   - Other journalists at the same event were not obstructed (para 32)
   - The force used (grabbing/pulling hands) caused injury (para 14)
   - Memory cards were allegedly damaged (para 15, 26)

   Case law references:
   - Need to consider 2594/07, 1172/12, 11882/10 (though we don't have their details, these would be relevant precedents)
   - Generally, ECtHR has been very protective of journalists' rights, especially when they're identifiable as press

   Assessment:
   - Was there a pressing social need? There was an altercation, but the applicant was a journalist covering it, not necessarily participating
   - Was this the least restrictive measure? Arresting a journalist and seizing equipment is highly restrictive. Police could have created a perimeter rather than interfering with the journalist
   - Convincing reasons? The domestic courts found no obstruction of journalistic activity (para 32), but this seems questionable given:
     * She was identifiable as a journalist
     * She was shouted at to let go of her camera
     * The video shows her resisting removal of her camera
     * Other journalists weren't obstructed

   - The interference seems disproportionate - the fact that she was a journalist should have been a major consideration
   - The seizure of equipment and damage to memory cards (if proven) would be particularly problematic

   Therefore: **The interference cannot be considered necessary in a democratic society**

**Conclusion:** There has been a violation of Article 10.
